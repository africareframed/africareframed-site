How it works

Find → collect → download → use

Images are always free for partners. Each download comes with its own licence: your permission in writing, for the use you chose.

Find

Search by keyword, theme and place

Open Browse images and use the search tab. Every image carries keywords for what it shows, its theme, its city and its country. A click on a keyword under an image shows every image that shares it.

The five themes are listed on the House Collection page, each with a link into the archive.

Collect and download

Gather a selection

Under an image, press Add to selection. Your selection is under Lightbox in the menu, and the selections you save are listed in My account.

Download an image

Press Download (free licence) and choose how you will use the image: in a report or publication, a funding application, a presentation, on your website or social media, in the press, at an exhibition or event, in teaching, or another use that you describe.

A comment on the project or the publication is welcome. The original file, 6,000 pixels wide, comes to your email together with the terms.

Why does each image come with a licence?

The licence is your permission in writing. It comes to your email with the image, for the use you chose, so you can always show a colleague, a publisher or a funder that you have the right to use it.

It is free, every time. A new use is a new download, with a licence of its own.

Each licence also shows where the images are used. That record is what makes new collections possible.

Use

What the licence covers, at a glance

It covers

  • The use you chose at download, anywhere in the world.
  • Cropping and colour adjustment.

It does not cover

  • Resale, or passing the licence on.
  • Political campaigning, or advertising for products.
  • Changes to what an image shows.
  • Training AI models.

Every use carries the credit line.

The people in the images are real. Show them in contexts they would recognise, with captions that are true.

Before you publish
  • The credit line stands on or beside the image.
  • The caption says what the image shows, and where.
  • The use is the one you chose at download. For a new use, download the image again.

Businesses built on waste

Waste is a raw material. Across East Africa, young companies build businesses on it: cooking fuel from husks and sugarcane, paving from plastic, paper bags from banana trunks, and an app that gets the rubbish collected. This collection shows seven of them, the people who run them and the work they do, in their own places.

The companies came through BESTSELLER FOUNDATION’s Waste to Value Challenge, launched with Intellecap in December 2019 across Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda. From more than 400 applications, six were chosen for an accelerator that ran from August 2020 to March 2021, with up to USD 100,000 each and support on markets, products, business planning and finance. Alongside it, the foundation invested in a seventh: Vuma Biofuels.

Waste to building materials

Paving stones from plastic waste

Gjenge Makers · Nairobi, Kenya · founded by Nzambi Matee

In a workshop in Nairobi’s Industrial Area, plastic waste from packaging factories and collectors is mixed with sand, heated and pressed into paving blocks and tiles, certified by the Kenya Bureau of Standards. The materials engineer Nzambi Matee founded the company in 2017 and was named a UN Environment Programme Young Champion of the Earth in 2020.

Since then: a gavel she made from recycled plastic was used at the UN Environment Assembly in 2022, and she joined the Explorers Club 50 in 2025.

Technology for waste

Booking the rubbish collection by app

Yo-Waste · Kampala, Uganda · founded by Martin Tumusiime

Homes and businesses in Kampala book their rubbish collection on a phone, on schedule or on demand, and the app sends an independent collector to the door. Yo-Waste began as a student project at Makerere University.

Since then: runner-up for the Royal Academy of Engineering’s Africa Prize for Engineering Innovation, 2024.

Waste to energy

Cooking fuel from organic waste

Gracela Ventures · Kampala, Uganda · founded by Grace Nalugwa

Organic waste is carbonised and pressed into briquettes that households, schools and hotels burn in place of charcoal and firewood. Grace Nalugwa started the business with a youth livelihood grant and built it in Kawempe, in the north of Kampala.

Since then: Grace Nalugwa has been a Mandela Washington Fellow, 2022, and a One Young World scholar, 2024.

Waste to packaging

Paper bags from banana trunks

Umuti · Kigali, Rwanda · founded by four students of the African Leadership University

In a country that banned plastic bags in 2008, Umuti set out to make paper ones from the banana and plantain trunks left in the fields after the harvest. When we photographed it in 2021, the young company was in its pilot phase, backed by the foundation.

Waste to energy

Used cooking oil, collected for fuel

Zijani · Nairobi, Kenya · founded by Bryan Lukano

When we photographed it in 2021, Zijani collected used cooking oil from hotels, restaurants and caterers in Nairobi, so that it went into biodiesel and other products instead of down the drain. Its founder first had the idea for a university assignment.

Waste to energy

Farm husks made into charcoal

Green Charcoal Uganda · Tororo, Uganda · founded by Geoffrey Okoth Yoga

When we photographed it in 2021, the company bought rice, coffee and palm kernel husks and maize cobs from smallholder farmers in eastern Uganda and turned them into cooking briquettes: a farm’s leftovers, sold as fuel.

Waste to energy

Sugarcane waste as fuel

Vuma Biofuels · Awendo, western Kenya · founded by Aaron Pattillo and Ian Otula

When we photographed it in 2021, Vuma turned the bagasse left over from sugarcane in western Kenya into briquettes and pellets: fuel for industrial boilers, schools and homes, in place of firewood.

From the first call with a founder to the last gallery.

    A brief with every founder

    The foundation and each founder got a gallery of their own, to use across all their channels.

A collection on your theme

This collection began with a theme close to the foundation’s own work: waste turned into value. A collection can start from any theme close to a funder’s work, or one it wants to support: a sector, a skill, a city, a change under way.

We find the people and places that show it, work with photographers from the countries concerned, and deliver the collection with its stories and its behind-the-scenes material. From there the storytelling is shared: the funder, the people in the collection and the press each tell their part with the same images. The funder’s name stands on the collection; the work stays independent.

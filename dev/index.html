What guides the work

What the work on Home made clear, to keep in sight while the other pages are built.

  • Why, what, howEach page answers one question. About: why this exists. The collections: what it is. Partnerships: how one takes part. Home answers all three, one part each, and each part leads to the page that answers the same question at length. Before a page is built, say which question it answers; every part of it serves that, or goes.
  • One order in every partThe title on the left. On the right a sentence, about three lines, and the way on. Then what the text opens.
  • Proof in its contextA name or a number stands where it explains the work, not where it only shows scale: who licensed the images, by sector; who supported the work, each with what it supported.
  • Draw only the order a reader can seeA structure nobody works out at a glance reads as noise or as a mistake. Leave it out and make the form whole.
  • The mood countsThe archive is the product; the mood is what sets it apart. Home is where the mood is set.
  • Words for a reader outside the tradeShe has an academic education and does not know the picture trade. A mechanism such as a licence is explained where it first stands. A button says where it leads.

How to read the wireframe

  • Grey box with a crossAn image still to choose, at the size and proportion it will have.
  • Grey barsText still to write.
  • Yellow noteWhat goes there, and where it comes from.
  • Clean viewIn the yellow strip: hides the notes and labels, so a page can be shown as a layout.
  • GridIn the yellow strip, or the key G, or ?grid in the address: draws the columns (blue) and margins (red) of the current width, with the values in a readout.
  • Module labelsEvery section carries the number and variant of the module it is built from (Website – modules); hidden in clean view. All modules with their variants: /dev/modules.
  • Draft copyText with a dotted underline is draft copy written from the masters, to be approved by Thomas; the mark hides in clean view.
